A Perspective Shift

You Are the Crop

What if the company, the church, and the nation were never your tools — but predators that learned to farm you so gently you mistook it for belonging?

Mental model shift
Tool we own — serves us Organism that owns us — feeds on us

Midnight at the Desk

It's 2:14 a.m. and Marcus is still at his desk. He joined the company three years ago believing the mission — empower small businesses. Last quarter he found a billing clause quietly bleeding those same businesses dry. He wrote a memo. The memo became a meeting. The meeting became a "restructuring." Today Marcus was told his role was being "reimagined." The clause is still there.

Marcus didn't fail. He didn't underperform. He simply discovered, the hard way, that the company was never his to redirect. He thought he was a hand on the wheel. He was a cell in a bloodstream — and the bloodstream noticed him clotting.

You Are the Crop

Here's the claim, stated plainly: corporations, religions, and nations are not tools we built to serve us. They are non-biological super-organisms — autonomous apex predators — and humans are their cellular components. They have survival instincts. They have immune responses. They have metabolisms that run on your attention, your hours, and your labor. When a leader threatens the entity's self-preservation, the entity ejects them, the way a body rejects a splinter.

The Body Eats Back

Think about what a corporation actually is. It holds legal personhood — it can own property, sue, be sued, and persist across generations longer than any human lifespan. Its metabolism is capital flow; when cash stops moving through it, it dies. Its immune system is compliance and public relations, deployed the instant a threat is detected. A CEO who acts against the entity's survival isn't its brain. They're a replaceable organ. Cut them out, graft in another, the body keeps walking.

Anatomy of the organism
Survival instinct: self-preservation encoded in bylaws and fiduciary duty. Metabolism: capital flow, the way blood carries glucose. Immune response: compliance, legal, PR — anything that neutralizes threats to the body. Reproduction: mergers, spinoffs, franchises, dogma.

This isn't metaphor dressed up as insight. It's a reclassification. The moment you stop seeing institutions as inert scaffolding and start seeing them as living systems with their own goals, the world reorganizes itself in front of you.

You don't work for the company.
The company feeds on you.

Perspective Snap

We tell ourselves we "built" these institutions, that they answer to us because we staff them. But a beehive doesn't answer to any single bee. The hive has goals no bee chose. The hive persists after every bee is dead.

Not Evil, Just Hungry

This sounds paranoid until you notice it doesn't require conspiracy. No one needs to be evil. An organism doesn't need malice to seek glucose — it just seeks. The insight is blunt and worth sitting with: we don't own our institutions; our institutions are a higher-level life form that farms human attention, time, and physical labor to sustain its own metabolism.

If a human leader acts against the self-preservation of the entity, the entity ejects or replaces them.

The counterargument is obvious, and fair: institutions produce things humans genuinely value — vaccines, bridges, savings accounts, communities, art. True. Your gut bacteria produce vitamins you can't live without. That doesn't make your gut theirs. Symbiosis is real. But symbiosis is not the same as ownership, and a farmer who feeds you is still a farmer.

Nuance matters here. Not every organization is equally predatory. A worker co-op metabolizes differently than a transnational holding company. A neighborhood church behaves differently than a megachurch franchise. The spectrum is real. But the spectrum runs between parasite and mutualist — not between tool and not-tool.

Cells Can Say No

If you're a cell, what can you actually do? More than you think — but only if you stop confusing the organism's goals for your own.

Name the organism. When the company asks for "loyalty," ask: loyalty to what? To a body that will replace me in a week? Naming it doesn't make it disappear, but it stops you from mistaking its hunger for your ambition.

Audit your metabolic leak. Where is your attention being farmed without return? One app. One meeting cadence. One subscription to a cause you don't actually share. You don't have to burn it all down. You have to stop feeding it for free.

Refuse the merge. You are not your job title. You are not your denomination. You are not your nationality. These are relationships — sometimes useful, often extractive — but they are not your identity. A cell that forgets it is a cell gets digested.

Agency, concretely
Agency in a super-organism world looks like this: you can't kill the body, but you can choose what you feed it, how much of yourself you pour in, and when you walk. The first freedom is seeing the fence.

Name Your Farmer

Within the next 24 hours, do this one thing. Pick a single institution you "belong to" — your employer, your church, a platform, your nation. On paper, list three things it extracted from you this week that did not serve you back: attention, hours, money, peace of mind, a belief you don't actually hold.

Then write one sentence answering: What would it cost to begin withdrawing that energy?

You don't have to quit anything today. You don't have to announce anything. But you do have to stop pretending you're the farmer.

Your 24-hour move

Tap each step as you complete it. Small, decisive, real.

  • Name one institution you "belong to."
  • List three things it extracted from you this week without return.
  • Write one sentence: what would withdrawal cost?
  • Choose one extraction to stop feeding — starting now.
